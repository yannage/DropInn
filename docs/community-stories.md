# Community story proposals

Future product direction; this workflow is not implemented yet.

A player should eventually be able to suggest a predicament, have it reviewed by a curator, and see it become a playable DropInn adventure. Keep the current MVP focused on a reliable five-minute visit before building a submission system.

## Proposed experience

1. Submit a short pitch: title, situation, desired tone, an interesting choice, and a possible payoff. A prose idea is enough; no game rules or model access required.
2. Receive a private submission receipt. Statuses: submitted, in review, needs revision, accepted for development, playtesting, published, or declined. Acceptance is not a promise of publication.
3. A curator reviews originality, suitability for cooperative public play, and whether the idea fits short drop-in chapters. Ask submitters to confirm permission to use their contribution and choose attribution; settle the actual submission terms before launch.
4. Convert accepted ideas into authored chapters with objectives, pressure, supported targets/effects, and success/mixed/setback endings. AI may help draft, but a curator approves the playable version.
5. Test newcomers, late arrivals, empty rooms, all-companion support, failed checks, and ten-round closure before publishing.
6. Publish an approved version to discovery with creator credit. Existing rooms retain their original version so edits cannot change a running game.

## Prerequisites

- Replace the global Briar Glen chapter list with a validated adventure registry and stable adventure/version IDs on rooms and recaps.
- Prove the registry with a second internally authored adventure before accepting public content.
- Store submissions separately from playable definitions; unpublished submissions never appear in public discovery.
- Require authenticated submitters, submission limits, and curator-only review/publish permissions.
- Keep an audit trail of revisions and review decisions. Support withdrawing unpublished pitches and unlisting published adventures from future discovery.
- Treat submitted prose as content, never executable code or privileged model instructions. Playable definitions use bounded effects and validated targets; stories cannot change XP rules, equal starting power, credentials, or server behavior.
- Establish who reviews the queue and how authors receive feedback before enabling submissions.

## First useful release

A simple pitch form, the author's status page, a curator queue, and manual conversion into an approved adventure definition. Defer public voting, automated publishing, creator payments, and a full visual story editor until the curated loop works.
